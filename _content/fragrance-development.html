Accords written for your brand, not pulled off a shelf

Fragrance development at First Perfumes begins in our Jebel Ali laboratory with a written brief and ends with a signed-off formula, an IFRA conformity certificate and a master batch record your production schedule can repeat. Perfumers and application chemists work on the same floor as compounding, so an accord is never designed in isolation from the vessel, the alcohol grade or the bottle it will eventually live in.

Most programmes run eight to fourteen weeks from brief to approved formula, depending on the number of submission rounds and whether the concentrate has to clear stability in more than one base. An Eau de Parfum, a body mist and a reed diffuser will each behave differently with the same accord, and we test all of them before anyone signs.

From bench to batch

A formula that behaves at two hundred grams on a bench does not always behave at four hundred kilos in a jacketed vessel. Every approved accord is trialled at pilot scale in the same compounding hall that will run the commercial batch, so maceration time, filtration temperature and settling behaviour are recorded before the first production order is scheduled. Nothing is handed to the line as a theory.

Development Path

From Written Brief To Approved Formula

Four stages, roughly twelve weeks, and a great deal of smelling. Nothing moves on until the stage before it is signed off.

Start a Brief
STEP : 01

Brief intake and olfactive direction

We start from a written brief covering the target consumer, price architecture, concentration, launch market and any reference the client wants the fragrance to sit beside. Where a brief is still forming, our team runs a direction session with a scent board of fifteen to twenty raw materials and accords so the vocabulary is agreed before anything is weighed. The output is a signed olfactive direction and a cost-per-kilo ceiling for the concentrate.

STEP : 02

Accord construction and bench trials

Perfumers build the accord in layers — top, heart and base are balanced separately before the composition is assembled. Trials are weighed to four decimal places, macerated for a minimum of seventy-two hours and evaluated blind on blotter and on skin by an internal panel. A first submission usually carries three to five directions rather than a single answer, so the brief can be tested from more than one angle.

STEP : 03

Stability and compatibility testing

The concentrate is dosed into the intended base and put on test: accelerated ageing at forty degrees, freeze-thaw cycling and light-box colour checks, run in parallel with compatibility against the actual bottle, pump, gasket and cap. We are looking for colour drift, precipitation, odour shift and any interaction with the closure. Results are issued as a written stability report with a recommended shelf life for the finished product.

STEP : 04

Client approval rounds and handover

Submissions ship as 30 ml and 100 ml samples in neutral packaging with an evaluation sheet. Two revision rounds are included in a standard development programme and most projects settle inside them; further rounds are quoted per submission. Approval is recorded against a formula number and a retained reference sample, and that sample becomes the standard every future production batch is measured against.

Naturals, synthetics and the ledger behind them

A modern accord is rarely all one thing or the other. Naturals bring depth, movement and a story a marketing team can actually tell; synthetics bring diffusion, tenacity, colour stability and a cost that survives a retail margin. Our palette is built deliberately from both, and every material we buy is traceable to a supplier specification and a delivered batch.

What we source, and how

  • Steam-distilled and expressed naturals from qualified origins
  • Absolutes, resinoids and CO2 extracts for depth and fixation
  • Oud and agarwood fractions assessed batch by batch
  • Aroma chemicals bought to supplier specification and purity
  • Approved alternates held for every volatile-supply material
  • Allergen and IFRA data collected before a material is used

Materials are quarantined on arrival, checked against their certificate of analysis and released by our quality team before a perfumer is allowed to weigh them. That single step is what makes a formula repeatable ten batches and five years later, which matters far more to a brand owner than any single clever trial.
